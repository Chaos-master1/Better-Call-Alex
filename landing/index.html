Questions

Asked by skeptical lawyers.

Does it practice law? Is the output legal advice?

No. Alex produces attorney work product in draft. Every document carries DRAFT — REQUIRES LICENSED REVIEW — NOT LEGAL ADVICE, applied in code, not by prompt suggestion. A licensed attorney reviews everything before it matters.

What happens when the AI is wrong?

The most important question. Wrong citations and quotes don't reach you as truth — the verifier rejects them and the UI renders them struck-through with the reason, so you see the boundary of what's proven. Silence would be the failure mode; visibility is the design.

How is this different from asking ChatGPT for cases?

A general model generates citations from training weights — fluent, confident, sometimes fictional (729+ courtroom incidents since 2023). Alex never generates a citation from weights: every cite resolves in a real corpus database and every quote is matched against the cited opinion's stored text, by code that has no model inside it.

What are the honest limitations?

Treatment signals are inferred from citing language, not an editorial citator — never stated as fact. Local models reason less deeply than frontier ones. Dollar figures are heuristic and labeled as such. US case law only, for now. These ship in the product, not just in fine print.

What hardware do I need?

A machine with ~12 GB of VRAM holds the resident model; the corpus database is ~170 GB on disk. No GPU farm, no cloud bill, no account.
